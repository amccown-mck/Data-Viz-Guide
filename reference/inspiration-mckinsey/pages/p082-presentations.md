## Intent
Explains how to structure a chart as a build-up sequence across presentation slides: first orient the audience to the chart, then reveal the insight, then show the complete picture. Also shows an alternative that de-emphasizes (masks) columns rather than hiding them. Uses the same "Australians are the world's most prolific gamblers" example as page 81.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Build-up sequences (left column): help viewers settle in by letting them get familiar with the content before delivering the insight.
- A sequence usually runs two to five steps; the exact number depends on the story, the chart type and how complex the data is.
- Step 1 – Set the scene: the order in which elements appear changes how the audience engages. Begin by framing: show everything needed to read the chart (axis, labels) plus only part of the data. Avoid showing all data at once; reveal it progressively.
- Step 2 – Highlight the insight: once the audience is engaged, emphasize the key message. The rest of the data can be shown, but greyed out so attention stays on the highlighted insight.
- Step 3 – Show the full picture: the final step shows the complete slide with all data. When further data categories are introduced, add their supporting key information (e.g., legend entries) only at the moment those categories appear.
- Alternative approaches: sometimes mask certain columns so they remain visible but read as secondary; they can then be fully revealed on the following slide.

## Examples marked Recommended
- No explicit Recommended / Avoid labels; all four panels are presented as good practice.

- Step 3 — full five-category stacked column chart (Online, Lotteries, Betting, Casino, Gaming machines) with full legend at right; Australia label bolded; title "Australians are the world's most prolific gamblers" + subtitle; a third subtitle line is cropped/illegible (likely "Top 10 countries" per page 81, not confirmed). — Complete picture after context and insight are established.
- Alternative approach — same full stacked chart, but all columns except Italy are rendered washed-out / semi-transparent with a stippled/dithered texture; Italy column in full-strength color with bold label; annotation above Italy: heading "The House wins in Italy" with short note that gaming machines were legalized only a few years ago and Italy's gaming machine industry is booming; short dark rule under/near annotation; five-category legend at right. — Masked columns stay visible as secondary context while one column is emphasized.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: dark navy = base/primary series (Gaming machines, and all columns in Step 1); light grey = de-emphasized "other" in Step 2; bright blue = highlight (Italy segment in Step 2) and Casino category in Step 3; royal blue = Betting; pale light blue = Lotteries; steel/teal blue = Online; washed-out stippled tones = masked secondary columns in Alternative.
- Axes: Step 1 has no y-axis ticks/gridlines (value labels replace them), just a 0 at baseline; Steps 2–3 and Alternative have thin light-grey horizontal gridlines at 0/250/500/750/1,000 with small grey tick labels at left; no vertical axis line.
- Labels: Step 1 uses direct value labels above bars (integers, no units, no separators: "1000"); later steps use a legend top-right with small square swatches; category labels under each column in small grey sans; focus category label bolded (Australia in Steps 1 & 3; Italy in Step 2 & Alternative).
- Sort: ascending by total left to right, consistent across all steps so position stays stable between slides.
- Bar width roughly equal to gap width or slightly wider; consistent spacing across steps.
- Typography: slide headline bold sans; subtitle regular sans; annotation heading bold small sans with smaller regular body lines; tick and category labels very small grey sans. ## Annotation & narrative patterns
- Insight headline kept constant across all build steps; subtitle provides metric, unit, year, currency.
- Callout annotation placed directly above the highlighted column with a bold short headline ("The House wins in Italy") plus 1–2 lines of explanatory context.
- Narrative progression: context -> highlight -> full detail; emphasis moves via color (bright blue vs grey) and bold labels.
- Legend entries introduced incrementally in step with the data categories.

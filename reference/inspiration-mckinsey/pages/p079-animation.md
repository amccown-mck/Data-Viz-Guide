## Intent
Chapter opener for motion: sets out the core principles for animating charts (bringing data to life with motion). Applies to any motion context — standalone animations or digital/interactive experiences. Four principle panels: entrances & sequencing, focusing & highlights, iterations/aggregations/partitions, transitions & exits.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Fundamental principles (left column):
  - The chapter covers principles and considerations for animating charts.
  - These principles hold for every kind of motion, whether in an animation or a digital product.
  - Visualizations should appear, animate and move between states in a simple, sophisticated way (cross-reference "pg XX" — placeholder, unresolved).
  - Motion should protect the viewer's comprehension at every moment (cross-reference "pg XX" — placeholder).
  - Italic note: digital editions of the guidelines will include animated examples.
- Entrances and sequencing: the order in which data and supporting elements appear helps viewers absorb information; plan the build order deliberately because it drives reading order.
- Focusing and highlights: unlike static charts, animation can steer attention to an insight by changing the "camera angle" (zooming/reframing), changing colors, or moving elements.
- Iterations, aggregations and partitions: a key benefit of motion is updating or changing data over time, which keeps each view simple and focused; elements can be merged to summarize or split apart to reveal detail.
- Transitions and exits: data can move between different axes or even different chart types; movement should be smooth and predictable so the viewer can follow it; exits should mirror the technique used when the chart first populated.

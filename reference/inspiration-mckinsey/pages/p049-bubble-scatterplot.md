## Intent
Introduces the bubble scatterplot as a correlation chart for showing how two or more variables relate. Each entity is one bubble: X and Y position carry two values and bubble size carries a third. Suited to social, economic, medical and scientific relationships. It's a variant of the dot scatterplot with bubbles in place of points.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Section definition (black box): correlation charts show how two or more variables relate to each other.
- A bubble scatterplot encodes three dimensions: X position, Y position and bubble size.
- It helps explain social, economic, medical and scientific relationships, and can be treated as a dot scatterplot that uses bubbles.
- Callout: shaded hatching over the relevant region of the plot draws attention to the core story.
- Callout: label only the bubbles that matter to the story/insight.
- Callout: coloring bubbles by category makes cluster trends and patterns visible.

## Examples marked Recommended
- Single worked example (not labeled Recommended/Avoid): countries with life expectancy above 78 years, 2017.
  - X = wealth (GDP per capita, $0–$80,000). Y = health (life expectancy, 78–84 years). Bubble size = population. Color = region.
  - A hatched grey band covers roughly $30,000–$50,000 (visually, between the $20,000 and $60,000 gridlines around the $40,000 tick) and is annotated "The average income line", with a note that most countries cluster around $20–40,000 per capita. Note: the band's position and the stated range don't quite match on the page.
  - Only one bubble carries a direct label: "US", a large royal-blue bubble at about $55–60K and about 79 years, which illustrates the headline.

## Intent
Explains scrolly-telling: content (e.g., a chart) appears or changes as the reader scrolls, to carry a narrative forward. Best suited to long-form explanatory pieces where the text walks the reader through complex stories or datasets. Contrasted with a long article containing embedded static graphics.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Scrolly-telling = revealing or changing content (such as a chart) as the user scrolls, in service of a narrative.
- Rather than a long article filled with embedded static charts, the visuals sit beside the text and update dynamically to reflect the insight currently being discussed.
- Use it for long-form explanatory work where the text leads the reader through complex stories or data.
- Mechanics (captions under the tablet mockups):
  - Text or article — scrolls along with the reader.
  - Data visualizations — stay pinned in place while scrolling but update to reflect the messages in the text.

## Examples marked Recommended
- No explicit markers. Example: a long vertical web article "The applications and value of deep learning" (subtitle about an analysis of 400+ use cases across 19 industries; illegible in detail) shown as a full-page strip: dark hero with aircraft image and serif headline → bright blue band "What are neural networks?" with two ring gauges (49%, 92%) → white section with treemap and text "Artificial intelligence stands out as a transformational technology of our digital age" → dark navy scrolly section (outlined in red and zoomed into two tablet mockups).
  - Tablet 1 (top state): left text column beginning "We collated and analyzed more than 400 use cases across 19 industries and nine business functions." Right: pinned bubble matrix "Annual investments in AI systems, 2010-2018", "USD in millions"; columns Automotive, Media, Insurance, Oil and Gas, Banking; rows 2010–2018; all dots white.
  - Tablet 2 (scrolled state): text has moved up; sentence about the automotive industry (invested the least in AI 2010–2018 but most consistently) is highlighted with bright blue background bars; the chart stays fixed and now shows the Automotive column in bright blue while other columns turn grey. Works because the visual emphasis synchronizes with the sentence being read.

## Examples marked Try to avoid / Not recommended
- Implied alternative (not explicitly marked): long article with embedded static graphics.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: dark navy background for the scrolly section; white dots = neutral/all categories; bright blue = highlighted category and highlighted text; mid grey = de-emphasized categories after highlight. Hero band in saturated electric blue with white text.
- Bubble matrix: dot area encodes investment; thin vertical lines under each column; year labels at right; column labels on top; small size key ("USD in millions") at top.
- Text highlight: bright blue background boxes behind the relevant lines, with the key phrase ("automotive industry") in bold.
- Ring gauges: thin circular strokes with large numeral percentages centered, small labels beneath (illegible).
- Treemap: dark navy largest block, bright blue and royal blue smaller blocks, tiny labels (illegible).
- Red connector lines/outline (presentation annotation) link the red-boxed article region to the zoomed tablet mockups.
- Typography: page title serif ("Long-form or "scrolly-telling""); article hero headline in serif; section and body text in sans; chart titles small bold sans; body text light and small.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- Narrative text drives the story; the chart acts as a persistent stage whose highlighting follows the paragraph in view.
- Bolded key term in text matches the highlighted series in the chart (text-to-chart color linkage via bright blue).
- Chart title descriptive with date range and units subtitle.

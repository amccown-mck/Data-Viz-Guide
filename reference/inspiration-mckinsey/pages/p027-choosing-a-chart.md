## Intent
Overview/selector page. It maps the analytical question to a family of charts. Use it as the entry point before choosing a specific chart. It sorts 18 chart types into 6 purpose-based groups (3 charts each).

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
Left column, "Types of charts":
- Picking the wrong visual, or reaching for the most familiar chart by default, can confuse the audience or lead them to read the data wrongly.
- To make charts that clarify and support analysis, first work out *why* you need a chart, given the data you have.
- Frame the purpose as a question. Are you comparing categories? Showing change over time? Showing geographic distribution?

Group definitions (paraphrased):
- **Magnitude**: for comparing size. The comparison can be relative (which is bigger) or absolute (by how much). Charts: Bar chart, Stacked bar chart, Bubble chart.
- **Correlation**: shows how two or more variables relate. Charts: Scatterplot, Dual axis chart, Heatmap.
- **Part-to-whole**: shows how parts are distributed and how one entity splits into its components. Charts: Donut and pie chart, Waterfall chart, Treemap.
- **Relationships**: shows logical links between entities, or how volume moves from one set to another. Charts: Sankey diagram, Parallel coordinates, Venn diagram.
- **Trend over time**: emphasizes change. The time span can be short (within a day) or long (decades to centuries). Charts: Line chart, Stacked area chart, Bubble timeline.
- **Geo-spatial**: for highlighting exact locations or geographic patterns. Charts: Choropleth, Bubble map, Dot density map.

## Visual specifics observed
- Layout: split page. On the left is a narrow off-white/white panel (about 20% width) with the serif page title at the top and the "Types of charts" body copy lower down. On the right is a light grey panel with a 2-column x 3-row grid of groups. Each group has a bold sans label, 1–2 lines of description, then 3 square thumbnails in a row with bold sans captions below.
- Group order: left column is Magnitude, Part-to-whole, Trend over time. Right column is Correlation, Relationships, Geo-spatial.
- Thumbnails: square tiles on a very dark navy (near-black) ground, with marks in white and a bright cyan/azure blue. So there are 2 accent colors plus the dark ground. White is the primary series and blue is the second series or highlight.
  - Bar chart: 4 white vertical bars of varying height.
  - Stacked bar: 4 bars, each white at the bottom with a blue top segment.
  - Bubble chart: white circles of various sizes.
  - Scatterplot: white dots on a blue-outlined grid (gridlines shown in blue).
  - Dual axis: one white line and one blue line crossing.
  - Heatmap: grid of small squares in white and blue (checkered/mixed).
  - Donut: ring, mostly blue with a white segment.
  - Waterfall: small white floating steps rising, ending in a blue total bar.
  - Treemap: one large blue rectangle plus smaller white rectangles.
  - Sankey: white and blue flowing bands crossing.
  - Parallel coordinates: vertical blue axes with white polylines.
  - Venn: two overlapping outline circles, one white and one blue.
  - Line chart: white line rising, with blue L-shaped axes (y and x baseline).
  - Stacked area: white mountain area over a blue area.
  - Bubble timeline: horizontal blue lines with white circles of different sizes placed along them.
  - Choropleth: US map with regions filled blue and white.
  - Bubble map: white US map with blue proportional circles.
  - Dot density map: white US outline/fill with small blue dots.
- Axes / gridlines: mostly omitted in the icons. They appear only where they help recognition (line chart axes, scatterplot grid).
- Typography: page title is a serif, about 2x the size of the group labels. Group labels and chart captions are bold sans. Descriptions and body copy are regular-weight sans in dark grey.
- Footer: same running footer as other pages. Page number 27 is at the bottom-right.

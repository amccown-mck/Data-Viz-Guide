## Intent
Introduces the "Trend over time" family and the line chart. Trend charts stress how values change; the span can be very short (within a day) or very long (decades/centuries). A line chart plots a series of points joined by segments at equal time intervals, and is preferred over bar charts when the story is the trajectory/trend rather than individual discrete quantities.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Use trend-over-time charts to emphasise changing patterns, at any time scale from intra-day to multi-century.
- A line chart shows how a dataset changes across equal time intervals; it foregrounds trend rather than discrete amounts (contrast with bar charts).
- Color the single most story-relevant line in the firm's deep/dark blue so it carries the insight; other series recede in lighter/other blues.
- Line stroke weight should be twice the gridline stroke weight (example: gridlines 0.75px, data lines 1.5px).
- Use a light grey shaded band to highlight the time segment containing the insight (the lightest grey in the palette).
- Stack series labels vertically at the right-hand end of the lines (direct labelling) to improve legibility instead of using a legend.

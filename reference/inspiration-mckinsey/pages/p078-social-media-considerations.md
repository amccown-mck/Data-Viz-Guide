## Intent
Gives best practices for adapting charts to social media, where screens are small and attention spans are short. The aim is digestible content that gets across the key insight rather than every detail. Hierarchy and highlighting are used to simplify the story so ideas land quickly or spark curiosity.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Where possible, design for smaller screens and shorter attention spans. Aim for easily digestible content that carries the insight, not every detail.
- Use hierarchy and highlighting to focus and simplify the narrative, either to communicate fast or to create intrigue.
- Simplify and clarify: if the whole dataset has to be shown for context, simplify how it is drawn so the insight stays the focus. Use highlighting, desaturation and direct labelling.
- Use platform-specific features: threads (Twitter) or Stories (Instagram) can tell a richer story through simple visuals shown in sequence, instead of showing all the data at once.
- Make each asset work on its own: include every necessary element (title, key/legend, sources, insight, brand mark) so the asset makes sense out of context and stays linked to the brand.
- Design for small screens: most social media is viewed on mobile, so the main insight must be readable at first glance without zooming.

## Examples marked Recommended
- "Simplify and clarify": a multi-series line chart with the x-axis labelled '60, '70, '80, '90, '00. Many context lines are drawn in very light grey, and one series is drawn as a dark navy line (zig-zag peaking around '90 and falling by '00). It works because the context stays visible but faded, so the eye goes to the single focal series.
- "Take advantage of unique platform features": a sequence of three square tiles labelled A, B and C, joined by a thin horizontal line to suggest a thread or story sequence. Tile A is a blue choropleth map (US counties, southern/central US visible) on black. Tile B is a white column chart on black with one bright-blue highlighted bar. Tile C is a blue/light-blue ring (donut) on black. It works because a complex story is split into simple, sequential frames.
- "Ensure the content works in isolation": the slope/rank chart titled "A huge spike in wages awards Los Angeles the number one spot for domestic purchasing power", with the subtitle "Top five cities ranked by DPP*, 2015–2018". The 2015 ranks are Zurich, Geneva, Luxembourg, Miami, Los Angeles. The 2018 ranks are Los Angeles, Zurich, Miami, Geneva, Luxembourg. Los Angeles is highlighted in bright blue. It includes a footnote/source line and the McKinsey & Company logo. It works because it is self-contained, with title, insight, source and brand mark all present.
- "Consider small screen sizes": a dark navy tile with a large "75%" inside a thick bright-blue progress ring (with a dark unfilled remainder). The small left-aligned label reads "Percentage of US workers who say they would work from home if they could". It has a faint source line and the logo. It works because the single number is readable immediately on a phone.

## Visual specifics observed
- Colour roles: dark navy is the focal line and tile backgrounds. Light grey marks context/de-emphasized series. Bright blue (#00a9f4-like) is the single highlight (focal bar, Los Angeles line and markers, KPI ring). Electric/deeper blue appears in the ring and map shades. Black is the background of the sequence tiles.
- Line chart: faint horizontal gridlines, a light baseline, and decade tick labels in the grey '60 format. No y-axis labels are visible. The focal line has a heavier stroke than the context lines.
- Column chart tile: plain white bars with one highlighted bar. No axes or labels.
- Slope chart: numbered circular rank markers, city names outside the markers, bold labels for the highlighted city, year headers above the columns, thin connecting lines, and a faint textured map background.
- KPI ring: the large numeral is in a light sans weight, with the ring arc showing proportion.
- Each example sits on a white card with a sans title, a thin divider and a blue check "Recommended" verdict with a caption.
- Typography: the page title is a large serif. The "Considerations" subhead is bold sans. Body text is small sans. Card titles are medium sans and captions are the smallest.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- The slope chart title is written as an insight headline, with a descriptive subtitle and an asterisked footnote defining DPP.
- The source line and McKinsey & Company logo appear on the social assets (bottom corners) so they stand alone.
- The sequence (A→B→C) acts as a narrative device for threads/Stories.

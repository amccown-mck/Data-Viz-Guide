## Intent
Shows how a chart embedded in an article should be adapted between a larger screen (tablet/desktop) and a mobile phone. Addresses the task of making the same chart readable on small screens.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- When adapting charts to mobile screen sizes, account for: format (aspect ratio), layout, text size, and repositioning of chart elements.
- (Implied by the example, not stated as rules) On mobile: shorten the headline, move direct end-of-line labels into a legend above the plot, move the annotation above the plot, and switch to a taller/narrower plot area.

## Examples marked Recommended
No explicit "Recommended"/"Not recommended" labels; both device renderings are shown as correct paired examples.
- Tablet/desktop article view — multi-line chart embedded between article paragraphs (serif body text above and below). Headline (2 lines): "While most energy source consumptions are steady, oil is more volatile as a consequence of economic shifts". Subtitle: US primary energy consumption, 2016–2018 (Quadrillion BTU). Seven series with direct labels at the right end of each line: Natural Gas, Oil, Nuclear, Coal, Wind, Solar, Tidal. A light grey vertical shaded band spans roughly Q4 '17 to Q1 '18, with an annotation above it: bold "Oil ramp up" plus a short note that oil consumption rose toward the end of 2017 due to changes in US tariff policy. Footnote placeholder line and "Source: mckinsey.com/insights" bottom-left; McKinsey & Company wordmark bottom-right. — Works on wide screens because there is horizontal room for end labels and a wide time axis.
- Mobile view — same chart re-flowed into a narrow portrait card. Headline shortened to "While most energy sources consumption are steady, oil is more volatile" over three lines; subtitle wraps to two lines. Direct labels replaced by a compact legend (two rows of short line swatches + series names) above the chart. "Oil ramp up" annotation moved above the plot area. A small "BTU" unit label and quarter tick labels appear at the top of the plot (x labels seemingly moved above the plot). Plot is taller relative to width; shaded highlight band retained. — Works because it preserves the key message and highlight while freeing horizontal space.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: dark navy/black line = Oil (the highlighted, volatile series); bright cyan/blue = Natural Gas; royal/medium blue = Nuclear; teal/steel blue = Coal; lighter pale blues/lavenders = Wind, Solar, Tidal; a faint pink/magenta line near zero (Tidal or an overlapping series—exact mapping partly illegible). Mostly a monochrome blue family with the focus series in the darkest value. Light grey shaded band = highlighted time period.
- Axes / gridlines: y-axis 0–4 with small grey tick numbers at 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 and faint horizontal gridlines; no y-axis line. X-axis labels: Q4 '16, Q1 '17, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q1 '18, Q2 (year shown only at the first quarter of each year).
- Labels: desktop = direct labels at the line ends, in small grey sans; mobile = legend above chart. Units given in subtitle (Quadrillion BTU); mobile also shows "BTU" at the axis.
- Stroke weights: thin, uniform line strokes; oil line visually darkest rather than thicker.
- Typography: page eyebrow "Format" small bold sans; page title "Screen sizes" large serif. Chart headline bold sans, subtitle regular sans (smaller), annotation title bold small sans with smaller regular note, footnote/source tiny grey sans. Surrounding article body in serif — chart uses sans, article uses serif.
- Layout: devices mocked up — a landscape tablet (left, large) and a smartphone overlapping at lower-right. Chart in tablet occupies a centered column narrower than the page, with article text above and below.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- Insight headline (states finding: steady sources vs. volatile oil, with cause) followed by descriptive subtitle with measure, period, and unit.
- Highlight band + short titled callout ("Oil ramp up") explaining the cause of the spike.
- Footnote placeholder then source line at bottom-left; brand wordmark bottom-right.
- On mobile, headline is trimmed to its core claim (causal clause dropped).

## Intent
Chart-selection taxonomy. Maps six analytical purposes to three recommended chart types each (18 total), shown as a grid of icon tiles, so the author picks a chart by the question being asked rather than by habit.

## Full structure / taxonomy (if any)
Layout: 2 columns x 3 rows of categories; each category = bold label + one-line definition + 3 icon tiles with captions.

| Category (position) | Question it answers (paraphrased) | Chart types |
|---|---|---|
| **Magnitude** (row 1, left) | How big are things relative to each other (which is larger) or in absolute terms (by how much)? | Bar chart; Stacked bar chart; Bubble chart |
| **Correlation** (row 1, right) | How do two or more variables relate to each other? | Scatterplot; Dual axis chart; Heatmap |
| **Part-to-whole** (row 2, left) | How is one entity split into its component pieces / how are the parts distributed? | Donut and pie chart; Waterfall chart; Treemap |
| **Relationships** (row 2, right) | What logical links exist between entities, or how does volume move from one set/state to another? | Sankey diagram; Parallel coordinates; Venn diagram |
| **Trend over time** (row 3, left) | How are values changing over time — from short intra-day moves to series spanning decades or centuries? | Line chart; Stacked area chart; Bubble timeline |
| **Geo-spatial** (row 3, right) | Where are things — precise locations or geographic patterns? | Choropleth; Bubble map; Dot density map |

Note: Waterfall is classed under Part-to-whole (not Magnitude). Dual axis chart is classed under Correlation. Venn is under Relationships.

## Guidance (paraphrased, exhaustive)
- Left-rail sub-head "Types of charts":
  - Picking an unsuitable visual — or reflexively using the most familiar chart — can confuse the audience or cause the data to be misread.
  - To produce charts that clarify and support analysis, first establish *why* a chart is needed given the data on hand.
  - Frame the purpose as a question: comparing categories? seeing trends over time? looking at geographic distribution?
- Category definitions (paraphrased):
  - Magnitude: use when comparing size; comparison may be relative (bigger/smaller) or absolute (the actual differences).
  - Part-to-whole: emphasize the distribution of parts and how one total decomposes into components.
  - Trend over time: emphasize change; span can be short (intra-day) or very long (decades/centuries).
  - Correlation: show the relationship between two or more variables.
  - Relationships: show logical relationships between entities, or volume flows from one set to another.
  - Geo-spatial: use when emphasizing exact locations or geographic patterns.

## Recommended examples — what & why
All 18 tiles are presented as appropriate choices for their category (no explicit do/don't on this page). Icon depictions:
- Bar chart: 4 white vertical bars of varying height.
- Stacked bar chart: 4 vertical bars, white base segment with blue top segment.
- Bubble chart: 5 white circles of differing size, clustered.
- Scatterplot: white dots over a blue grid (3x3 cells).
- Dual axis chart: two lines — one white, one blue — crossing.
- Heatmap: ~7x7 grid of small squares, white with scattered blue cells.
- Donut and pie chart: ring, mostly blue arc with a white remainder segment.
- Waterfall chart: floating white steps rising left→right, ending with a taller blue total bar.
- Treemap: large blue rectangle top-left, white rectangles filling remaining space.
- Sankey diagram: white and blue curved flow bands crossing.
- Parallel coordinates: 4 blue vertical axes with white/blue polylines crossing between them.
- Venn diagram: two overlapping outline circles (one white, one blue).
- Line chart: blue L-shaped axes, single white rising line.
- Stacked area chart: white area layered over blue area (mountain shapes).
- Bubble timeline: 3 horizontal blue lines with white circles of varying size placed along them.
- Choropleth: US map, white states with some filled blue.
- Bubble map: US map (white) with blue circles of varying size.
- Dot density map: US map (white) with many small blue dots.

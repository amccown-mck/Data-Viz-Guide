## Intent
- Show how one whole entity divides into its component parts (composition / distribution of shares).
- Use a donut or pie when the reader needs to see each part's relationship to the whole; keep to a small number of categories (five at most).
- A donut is preferred over a pie: it does the same job, and its empty center can hold an extra, related statistic or the key takeaway.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Section definition (black callout box): part-to-whole charts focus on how the parts are distributed and how one entity breaks down into its components.
- Donut charts are an effective way to show composition, meaning how the parts relate to the whole.
- Show no more than five categories per chart.
- A donut works the same way as a pie, except that it has a blank center and can support several statistics at once.
- Donuts give a better data-ink ("data intensity") ratio than standard pies because the empty center can display more related data.
- Annotation (left of chart): order wedges from largest to smallest. A callout fact in the center can add context about the main insight.
- Annotation (right of chart): place labels and values tidily outside the chart and in a meaningful order.

## Examples marked Recommended
- There are no explicit "Recommended" or "Not recommended" tags. The single example is presented as the model to follow.
- Donut chart titled "Who do people blame most for what's gone wrong in Washington?", subtitle "Distribution of preferences, 2017 (results from a survey of 906 adults)". It has 4 segments: Republicans in Congress 51%, President Obama 28%, Democrats in Congress 17%, Not sure 3%. The center text reads "Republicans face more blame than Democrats". It works because the wedges are sorted, there are few categories, the center states the takeaway, and the legend and values are aligned outside the ring in the same order as the wedges.

## Visual specifics observed
- Colors: 4 in total.
  - Near-black dark navy: largest segment (51%).
  - Bright cyan/sky blue: second segment (28%).
  - Saturated royal/electric blue: third segment (17%).
  - Light grey: "Not sure" (3%), shown as a residual or non-answer.
- Ring: thin stroke-style donut with a large hole (roughly 90%+ of the radius). Segments are separated by small white gaps.
- Wedge order: the largest segment starts at 12 o'clock and runs clockwise, followed by the smaller segments in decreasing order. The grey "Not sure" segment ends just before 12 o'clock.
- Legend: sits to the right of the donut as a vertical list. Each row has a small filled circle marker, a category name in regular weight, and a right-aligned bold percentage in its own column. The legend order matches the wedge order.
- Numbers: whole-number percentages with a % sign. No decimals.
- Center text: 2 lines, centered, in a regular-weight sans serif at a size between the subtitle and the headline.
- Axes and gridlines: none.
- A thin hairline rule separates the chart area from the footnote/source block.
- Card: the chart sits on a white card with a soft drop shadow, on a light grey page background.
- Typography:
  - Page title is a large serif ("Donut and pie chart").
  - Section eyebrow is a small bold sans serif ("Part-to-whole").
  - Body guidance text is a regular sans serif.
  - Chart headline is a bold sans serif phrased as a question.
  - Chart subtitle is a lighter, smaller sans serif.
  - Legend text and footnotes are smaller still; footnote and source text is the smallest and grey.
- Annotations sit on each side of the chart card, attached by thin horizontal leader lines.

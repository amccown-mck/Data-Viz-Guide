## Intent
Introduces the bubble timeline, which shows discrete events or items over time with a second quantity encoded as bubble area. Suited to comparing when events happened and how large they were across several categories.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- A bubble timeline places events/items on a time axis and encodes a variable as bubble area.
- It combines two techniques: a scaled timeline and a proportional-area chart.
- Callout: each data category gets its own horizontal line (row). You can compare across categories or read one category on its own.
- Callout: time runs along the X axis.
- Callout: a highlighted grey band marks the part of the chart that supports the headline insight.

## Examples marked Recommended
- Single worked example (not labeled Recommended/Avoid). Personal record breaches by industry, 2000–2014.
  - Five category rows (Technology, Financial, Retail, Government, Healthcare and education), each a faint horizontal line with dark navy bubbles sized by breach scale.
  - A shaded grey band over 2011–2014, with an annotation saying that since 2011 methods such as two-factor authentication have sharply cut breach frequency, except in finance.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: all bubbles are one dark navy/near-black color. A light grey rectangle highlights the insight period. Some bubbles contain small white hollow circles, which appear to mark overlapping/nested events (exact meaning not stated on the page).
- Axes / gridlines: the X axis is years shown as abbreviated two-digit labels with an apostrophe ('00 through '14) in small light-grey sans, and there's no axis line. Each category row has a thin light-grey guide line. No Y axis; category names act as row labels.
- Labels: category names are left-aligned to the left of the rows in small regular sans. The size legend sits at the top-left under the subtitle: two overlapping grey circles with the text "Scale of breach, millions of records". Units appear in the legend and are not repeated on the data.
- Sort order: rows follow no obvious magnitude order (Technology, Financial, Retail, Government, Healthcare and education).
- Shapes: filled circles with area proportional to value, ranging from tiny dots to one very large bubble (Technology around '09). Bubbles may overlap.
- Annotation placement: the insight note sits above the highlighted band and is aligned to it, with a thin dark rule at the top of the band.
- Typography: the page title is a large black serif ("Bubble timeline") with the small sans eyebrow "Trend over time". Chart headline is bold sans, two lines. Subtitle is a larger light/regular sans giving the metric and date range. Labels and annotations are small sans. Footnote and source are very small grey sans.
- Chart card: white card with a drop shadow on a light grey background. The left column is a white sidebar holding the description text. Callout notes sit outside the card and connect to chart features with thin grey horizontal leader lines.
- Footer: McKinsey & Company · Data Visualization Guidelines · Version 01 · 19.07.18 · page 47.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- The headline states the insight: breaches are slowing thanks to security technology, while finance remains a problem. A descriptive subtitle (metric + period) follows it.
- A grey shaded region plus a short text annotation ties the headline to a specific part of the data.
- Footnote placeholder line ("footnotes, explanations or methodology go here") and a "Source:" line sit below a thin divider at the bottom of the chart card.

## Other notes
- The left sidebar has two short descriptive paragraphs. Three explanatory callouts use leader lines: two on the left (categories on lines; time on X axis) and one on the right (highlighted grey area).

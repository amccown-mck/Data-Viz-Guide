## Intent
Sets the motion principles and anti-patterns for animated data sequences, so that motion reflects brand values: simple, human, purposeful. Five recommended behaviors and three to avoid, each shown on its own card.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Sophisticated and simple (left column): animated data sequences should follow defined principles that reflect the brand's values: simple, human and purposeful.
- Easing: objects should speed up and slow down naturally, like physical objects, so viewers can anticipate where they are going.
- Sequencing: bring elements in using a logical order so the viewer has time to take in each piece.
- Natural movements: motion should feel natural and fluid; lines should "draw" in from left to right; bubbles should grow outward from their center.
- Say what you see: make one point at a time; any voice-over or accompanying text must match what the data is doing on screen, because viewers struggle to hear and read different things at once.
- Keep it simple: split complex charts into simpler, more digestible sequences, or address a single point at a time.
- Overshoot and bounce (avoid): don't let objects go past their final size/position before settling for effect; this tends to look cartoonish or too playful.
- Animating without purpose (avoid): motion must do a job (e.g., show change over time or a transition); avoid passive animation that is only decorative.
- Distractions (avoid): keep it simple; too many moving objects make it hard to follow the narrative; avoid mechanical or distracting motion.

## Examples marked Recommended
- Easing — a bright-blue ease-out curve on L-shaped axes (steep rise that flattens toward the top right) — represents natural acceleration/deceleration.
- Sequencing — bubble scatter on a light grid (3x3 cells, short tick marks on axes) with four bright-blue bubbles of varying sizes — elements appear in a logical order.
- Natural movements — two jagged line series (one dark navy trending down, one bright blue trending up, crossing mid-chart), no visible axes, faint baseline — lines drawing left to right.
- Say what you see — one large bright-blue central circle surrounded by four small navy dots, with thin faint grey arrows at upper-left and lower-right pointing toward the center — focus on one point at a time.
- Keep it simple — three columns: two dark navy (second tallest), third shorter in bright blue — single highlighted takeaway, digestible.

## Examples marked Try to avoid / Not recommended
- Overshoot and bounce ("Avoid") — red/coral dotted trajectory arcs of a ball bouncing, ending in a solid coral dot — cartoon-like, overly playful.
- Animating without purpose ("Avoid") — 2x2 group of four identical large coral circles — decorative motion with no data meaning.
- Distractions ("Avoid") — navy center dot with several concentric thin coral orbit arcs with small coral dots moving on them — too many moving objects, mechanical/distracting.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: bright blue = recommended/featured data and highlight; dark navy = supporting data; coral/red-pink = used exclusively in "Avoid" examples; light grey = gridlines/arrows.
- Recommended badge: blue filled circle with white check + "Recommended" bold label; Avoid badge: coral filled circle with dark x + "Avoid" label; both bottom-left of each card.
- Axes: easing panel has thin dark L-shaped axes; sequencing panel has light grey grid with short dark tick marks outside plot; line panel has no y-axis, faint baseline only; other panels no axes.
- No data labels, legends, or numbers on any card.
- Layout: left sidebar + 4x2 grid of white portrait cards on light grey background; top row all Recommended, bottom row = 1 Recommended + 3 Avoid.
- Typography: "Format" eyebrow small sans; "Animation" large serif title; card headings bold sans; body copy light sans, ~3–4 lines.

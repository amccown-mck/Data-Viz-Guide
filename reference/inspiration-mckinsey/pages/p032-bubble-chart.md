## Intent
Compare the relative sizes of quantities at a glance. Best when values differ by large amounts and precise comparison of small differences is not needed. Not suitable when readers must judge fine differences, because area is harder to compare than length.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Bubble charts compare proportions (sizes, amounts, etc.) to give a quick sense of relative magnitude.
- Bubbles encode value by area, not length. Use them when values vary widely and fine differences are unimportant.
- Scale bubbles by area, not diameter.
- Example ordering is largest to smallest. The order can be changed to fit the layout or chart format.
- Bubbles in the example share one color. An alternative is to show the highlighted bubble (Breast cancer) in the brand "Deep Blue" and all other bubbles in gray.
- Bubble charts lend themselves to direct labeling because they have no gridlines or axes.

## Visual specifics observed
- Example chart: "Money raised to fight deadliest diseases, 2017 (USD)" with eight bubbles: Breast cancer 257m, Prostate cancer 147m, Heart disease 54m, Motor Neurone disease 23m, HIV 14m, Chronic Pulmonary disease 7m, Diabetes 4m, Suicide 3m.
- Color roles: the largest bubble (Breast cancer) is bright/cyan blue as the headline subject. All other bubbles are dark navy, so the chart uses two colors. This differs from the margin note, which says bubbles share one color. The image shows one highlighted bubble.
- Layout: bubbles are loosely arranged left to right roughly from largest to smallest, staggered on two rows with no axes, gridlines, or legend.
- Labels: direct labels use a bold value (e.g., "257m") above a regular-weight category name. Labels sit inside the bubble when it is large enough (white text on the navy bubbles; dark text on the cyan bubble) and below the bubble when it is small. Values use the lowercase "m" suffix for millions with no currency symbol, and units (USD) appear in the subtitle.
- Card: a white card with a soft drop shadow on a light grey page. A hairline rule separates the chart from the footnote area.
- Margin annotations: the notes sit left and right of the card, each attached by a thin horizontal leader rule pointing toward the chart.
- Typography: the chart headline is a bold sans set on two lines at a large size. The subtitle is regular sans, slightly smaller. Data labels are small, the footnote/source lines are very small and light grey, and the page title "Bubble chart" is a serif.
- Page layout: a white left rail holds the eyebrow, serif title, and two body paragraphs. The main area is grey with the centered example card.
- Footer: "McKinsey & Company", "Data Visualization Guidelines", "Version 01", "19.07.18", page "32".

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- The headline states the insight: breast cancer research receives more money than all other listed diseases combined. It is a full-sentence takeaway, not a chart description.
- A descriptive subtitle gives the measure, year, and currency unit.
- Footnote placeholder text marked with an asterisk ("*Footnotes, explanations or additional details of methodology go here…") is followed by "Source: mckinsey.com/insights", both in small grey text below a rule at the card bottom.
- The highlight color ties the bubble to the headline subject.

## Other notes
- Data check: 257m is more than the sum of the other seven values (252m), so the headline is consistent with the data.
- Named brand color referenced: "Deep Blue" (for a single highlighted bubble), with gray for the rest.

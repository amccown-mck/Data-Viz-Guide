## Intent
Shows how large, "featured" charts can be laid out in print publications — occupying a full page or spreading across a two-page spread. Use for report/magazine-style editorial layouts where a chart is a hero element alongside long-form text.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Left-rail subhead: "Featured charts".
- Large-scale charts can take up a single page or run across a double-page spread.
- (No other explicit rules stated; the page is a visual mock-up of print spreads.)

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: beeswarm uses dark navy/black dots on white; map uses a sequential blue ramp (light cyan = younger, dark navy = older), single-hue sequential.
- Axes: beeswarm has tiny top-axis labels (partly legible, roughly "100% female only" at left and "Parity 50%" further right), faint vertical reference lines; no heavy gridlines. Map has no axes.
- Labels: beeswarm row category names small above large serif numbers (410, 149, 197) placed at left of each row — direct labeling. Map uses a small vertical legend at top-left of the map and a direct callout with zoom circle.
- Charts are large and bleed across the gutter (beeswarm) or off the outer page edge (map) for an editorial, large-scale feel.
- Footnotes/sources: tiny footnote lines below the beeswarm and at the bottom of spread 2's left page (illegible).
- Typography: page titles and editorial headlines in large serif (e.g. "A big increase in online conversion", "Transforming sales capabilities"); lede in bold serif; body text small sans; chart headlines bold sans with lighter sans subtitle; row values in serif.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- Chart headlines are insight statements ("...only just starting to recover...", "...ageing quicker than the rest of the US"), followed by a descriptive subtitle with metric, geography, year range, and unit in parentheses.
- Callout with magnified inset circle + bold label + short explanation.
- Footnote/source line set small at the bottom-left of the chart area.

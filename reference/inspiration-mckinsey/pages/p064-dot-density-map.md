## Intent
- A dot density map uses dots, or another symbol, to show where a feature or phenomenon is present.
- Use it to show the spread and concentration of discrete occurrences across geography, such as store locations.
- The page gives no "do not use" guidance.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Many dots in an area mean a high concentration of the chosen field. Few dots mean a low concentration.
- A dot can stand for one occurrence (one-to-one) or for a fixed quantity (one-to-many).
- Side annotation: dots are placed at accurate positions on the map, so readers get a visual overview of the geographic distribution (here, of stores).

## Examples marked Recommended
- The page has no explicit "Recommended" or "Try to avoid" label. It shows one exemplar chart.
- The exemplar is three small-multiple US maps, one per grocery brand (Walmart, Albertsons, Safeway), with one dot per store location. Each map has its total count directly beneath it. The side-by-side layout makes the regional contrast easy to see: Walmart is concentrated in the East and Central US, while Albertsons and Safeway are concentrated in the West.

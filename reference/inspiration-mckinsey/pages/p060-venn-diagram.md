## Intent
Shows how sets relate and overlap, for example which roles sit where combined competency areas meet. Use it for conceptual or categorical set relationships, not for precise quantities.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Venn diagrams are a good way to show how sets are structured and how they relate.
- The overlapping shapes get darker as more of them overlap.
- Descriptions of the overlap areas go outside the diagram so the circles don't get overloaded with content.
- The single circles use the lighter brand "Active Blue". Where circles overlap, the colors combine: "Electric Blue" for two-way overlaps and "Deep Blue" for the darkest (three-way) area.

## Examples marked Recommended
- A single example chart, not explicitly labeled but shown as the model. It is a 3-circle Venn of an analytics team structure. The circles are Analytics (top), IT (bottom left) and Business decision making (bottom right). The pairwise overlaps are labeled Data scientist (Analytics∩IT), Analytics consultant (Analytics∩Business) and Data strategist (IT∩Business). The center is labeled Head of analytics. This works because the color deepens with each level of overlap and the descriptions are kept outside the shapes.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles, in 3 tones: bright sky/cyan blue (Active Blue) = single sets; saturated royal/electric blue = two-way overlaps; near-black deep navy = three-way center. There are no strokes or outlines on the circles.
- Labels: set names are bold and dark (navy/black) inside the single-set areas. The two-way overlap labels are regular-weight white text. The center label is bold white.
- External descriptions: three short descriptive texts sit to the left and right of the diagram. Each has a thin dark horizontal leader line above it that runs toward the overlap it describes. Left upper text: models/algorithms and supporting internal customers (→ Data scientist). Left lower text: data requirements and delivery roadmap (→ Data strategist). Right text, right-aligned: statistics and analytics understanding used in business decisions (→ Analytics consultant).
- The circles are equal in size and not scaled to any quantity.
- No axes or gridlines.
- Chart card: white panel with a soft shadow on a light grey page. A thin rule sits above the footnote.
- Typography: the page title is in serif. The chart headline is bold sans and large, the subtitle is regular sans and smaller, the annotation text is small regular sans, and the footnote/source is very small grey sans.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- The headline states an insight/claim: "The foundations of a balanced analytics team relies on data understanding" (grammar as printed). The subtitle is descriptive: "Analytics team core structure".
- Descriptions sit outside the diagram with leader lines.
- Footnote placeholder with an asterisk, then "Source: mckinsey.com/insights".

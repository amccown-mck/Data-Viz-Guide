## Intent
Explains when infographics are appropriate: they are a common, widely used format and work well for reaching broad, non-specialist audiences through social media or other public-facing channels. Icons can be used—sparingly and tastefully—to reinforce the key message and make content more appealing to a general public. Implied: better suited to external/social communication than to dense analytical decks.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Infographics are a popular, widely used device in data visualization.
- Use them primarily when targeting wider audiences via social media or other public channels.
- Infographics may include icons; icons should be used in a smart, elegant (restrained) way.
- The purpose of icons is to highlight the message and increase appeal to a broader public.
- Side note (card 1): an icon can serve as a tally mark (one icon = a fixed unit) to communicate quantities effectively.
- Side note (card 2): icons can be used to flag relevant categories that tie to the insight; especially useful on social-media assets where content must stand out visually.

## Examples marked Recommended
No explicit "Recommended"/"Not recommended" labels on this page; both examples are presented as positive exemplars.
- Pictogram / tally chart: "The United States holds a disproportionate amount of the world's richest people" (subtitle: the world's richest 1%, 2017). 30 person icons in a 3-row × 10-column grid; 16 are dark (row 1: all 10; row 2: first 6) and 14 are light grey. Legend key at right: one person icon = 1m people. Annotation block to the right, bold lead-in "A rich, rich country" followed by short explanatory text (over half of the world's richest people live in the US; roughly $34,000/year after tax puts you in the global top 1%). — Works because each icon is a countable unit, the dark vs grey split makes the share instantly visible, and the icon reinforces the "people" subject.
- Proportional circle comparison on dark navy card: "Google is focusing on electric cars" (subtitle: number of patents filed by Google, 2017). Four white circles scaled by value, increasing left to right: Laptops 3,725 (tiny dot), "Werables" [sic] 6,374 (small circle), Mobile 15,360 (large circle containing a phone line icon), Electric cars 25,544 (very large circle cropped by the card edge, containing a car line icon). — Works because sizes escalate toward the key category, and icons are placed only in the large circles relevant to the insight; the cropped largest circle dramatizes scale.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: card 1 on white — near-black/dark navy icons = highlighted subset (US share), very light grey icons = remainder/context. Card 2 inverts: dark navy background with white circles and white text; icons drawn as thin dark line art inside white circles. Effectively 2 colors per card (navy + white/grey).
- Axes / gridlines / baseline: none; no axes. Card 1 has a thin horizontal rule above the source line; card 2 has a thin light rule above the source line.
- Labels: direct labels only. Card 2: bold number above regular-weight category name, centered beneath (or within/beside) each circle; numbers use comma thousands separators, no units in label (unit in subtitle). Card 1: small legend key (single icon + "1m people") top-right of the grid instead of per-icon labels.
- Sort order: card 2 sorted ascending left to right, largest on the right edge. Card 1 filled left-to-right, top-to-bottom.
- Shapes / strokes: solid filled person pictograms (uniform size, evenly spaced); card 2 uses filled circles with thin-stroke outline icons (phone, car).
- Typography: page title "Infographics" in large serif; eyebrow "Infographics and icons" small bold sans above it; body text regular sans. Chart titles bold sans, subtitles regular sans, smaller; annotation lead-in bold sans with regular body; source line very small light grey sans ("Source: mckinsey.com/insights"). Card 2 value labels bold, category names regular, both larger than typical chart labels.
- Layout: left rail with section eyebrow, serif title, guidance paragraphs; right canvas with two overlapping example cards (white card upper-left, dark navy card lower-right overlapping) and small grey side notes connected by thin horizontal leader lines.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- Headlines are insight statements ("…holds a disproportionate amount…", "…is focusing on electric cars"), followed by a descriptive subtitle stating measure and year.
- Card 1 uses an embedded text callout with a short bold kicker and 2–3 sentence explanation beside the chart.
- Source line in small grey text at the bottom-left of each card, separated by a hairline rule.
- Explanatory meta-notes for the guide itself sit outside the cards in small grey text with leader lines.

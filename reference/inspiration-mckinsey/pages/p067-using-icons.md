## Intent
Covers when and how to use icons in charts and infographics. Icons can speed comprehension and offer a shortcut into the story, but should be used cautiously: if the message is clear without an icon, the icon is probably unnecessary.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Icons can help represent information so it is understood quickly.
- Iconography can shortcut the reader into the story, but treat it with caution.
- General rule: if something can be communicated clearly without an icon, don't add one.
- (Card: Convey quantities with icons) Literal icons used as tally units work as functional elements when they serve a simple job—helping the reader compare—inside an infographic.
- (Card: Categorize attributes) Icons can give a quick overview of complex data; use them to flag categories, or pair them with color coding to give meaning to different attributes.
- (Card: Improper scaling of icons) Do not size icons incorrectly. A frequent error is scaling an icon in both width and height to represent a value, which distorts the visual comparison and obscures the relationship to the data.
- (Card: Show variations with symbols) Simple icons can act as symbols to signal increases or decreases within a visualization.

## Examples marked Recommended
- "Convey quantities with icons" — 20 person pictograms in 2 rows × 10. Row 1: 10 dark navy figures (label at right: 10 people prefer cold). Row 2: 6 pink/red figures + 4 light grey figures (labels: 6 people prefer hot; 4 people prefer neither). Small caption above: temperature preference on 20 test subjects. — Works because each icon = one person, making counts directly comparable; color separates groups.
- "Categorize attributes" — six circles of varying size, each with a white line icon inside; legend top-left: Tech (dark navy dot), Health (bright blue dot). Navy (Tech) circles: monitor/desktop (small), battery (small-medium), phone (medium-large), airplane (largest). Bright blue (Health) circles: tooth (medium), heart with plus sign (medium-large). Tiny grey placeholder label lines under each circle (text illegible / placeholder). — Works because icons identify each item at a glance and color encodes category membership.
- "Show variations with symbols" — single dark line chart (≈19 points, no axes labels) with a thin baseline; beneath each x position a small triangle: upward dark triangle for an increase, downward pink/red triangle for a decrease. Observed sequence left→right: ▲▲▲▲▼▲▼▼▲▲▼▼▲▲▲▲▼▲▼. — Works because minimal symbols encode direction of change without extra text.

## Examples marked Try to avoid / Not recommended
- "Improper scaling of icons" (marked Avoid) — a single large person icon placed against a 0–20 y-axis (ticks 0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20 with light gridlines), segmented vertically: navy top portion (approx. 20→10), pink middle (approx. 10→4), light grey bottom (approx. 4→0). Text labels at right colored to match: 10 people prefer cold (dark), 6 people prefer hot (pink), 4 people prefer neither (grey). Caption: temperature preference on 20 test subjects. — Fails because the icon's shape/area (head, torso, legs of unequal widths) does not map proportionally to the values, so segment sizes mislead; scaling icons in two dimensions exaggerates differences.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: dark navy = primary group / Tech category / increases; pink-red (coral) = contrasting group / decreases; bright blue (cyan) = second category (Health); light grey = remainder/"neither"/de-emphasis. Max 3 colors per example.
- Axes / gridlines: only the Avoid example shows a y-axis with light grey gridlines and grey tick numbers. Line chart has only a hairline baseline, no gridlines or axis labels. Pictogram and bubble examples have no axes.
- Labels: direct labels to the right of rows (pictogram) and to the right of chart (Avoid example), label text colored to match series in Avoid example; small legend with colored dots for the bubble example. Plain-language counts ("10 people prefer cold").
- Shapes / strokes: solid filled pictograms, evenly spaced; filled circles with thin white outline icons; line chart uses a thin dark stroke; small solid triangles as change markers.
- Typography: eyebrow "Infographics and icons" small bold sans; page title "Using icons" large serif; body copy regular sans. Card titles bold sans; captions and labels small regular sans; verdict label ("Recommended"/"Avoid") bold sans with a small icon (blue check circle / red X circle) followed by regular-weight explanation.
- Layout: left rail guidance; right area a 2×2 grid of equal cards, each with a title, visual, hairline divider, and verdict + explanation.

## Intent
Dual axis charts display two data series that differ in magnitude and/or unit of measure, typically so the reader can compare two trends against each other over the same time span.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Use a dual axis chart for two series with different scales or measures; the usual purpose is trend comparison.
- Give each Y-axis its own text label plus a small colored "line icon" key so readers can link each line to the correct axis.
- Drop horizontal gridlines, because with two scales they would be ambiguous (they can only align to one axis).
- Both Y-axes must start at a zero baseline so the chart does not mislead or distort the data, particularly since the two series measure completely different things.

## Examples marked Recommended
- Single annotated exemplar (no explicit Recommended/Avoid labels): line chart "London is getting warmer and drier" — average yearly temperature (°C, left axis) vs rainfall (mm, right axis), 1987–2017. Demonstrates axis labels with line icons, no horizontal gridlines, zero baselines on both axes, and a highlighted period callout.

## Examples marked Try to avoid / Not recommended
- None shown on this page.

## Visual specifics observed
- Colors: bright/sky blue line = average temperature; dark navy line = rainfall. Two colors only. Highlighted period (2002–2005) shown as a light grey shaded vertical band with a dark rule across its top edge (band appears slightly textured/dithered in the capture).
- Axes: left Y-axis 0, 5, 10, 15 (temperature); right Y-axis 0, 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 (rainfall). Both start at 0. Tick numbers small light grey, no axis title on the axis itself — labels sit above each axis.
- Gridlines: thin light vertical gridlines at each x tick; no horizontal gridlines.
- X-axis: abbreviated years with apostrophe at 3-year intervals: '87, '90, '93, '96, '99, '02, '05, '08, '11, '14, '17.
- Series keys: small zig-zag line icon above each axis label — top-left icon in blue over "Average temperature (°C)", top-right icon in navy over "Rainfall (mm)" (right-aligned).
- Lines: thin, uniform stroke, no markers.
- Layout: chart sits in a white card with soft drop shadow on a light grey page; left column is a white sidebar with section label, serif title, and intro paragraph. Annotation notes sit in the margins left and right of the card, each attached by a thin horizontal leader rule.
- Typography: page title large serif; chart headline bold sans; subtitle regular sans, larger than labels; axis labels and callouts small sans; footnote/source smallest light grey.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- Chart headline is an insight statement ("London is getting warmer and drier"), followed by a descriptive subtitle stating measure, place and date range.
- In-chart callout: bold short heading "A wet year" plus one-sentence explanation positioned above the shaded 2002–2005 band.
- Footnote block below a thin rule: placeholder asterisked footnote line for explanations/methodology, then "Source: mckinsey.com/insights".
- Margin annotations (guide commentary) explain the design choices: axis labeling with line icons, omitted horizontal gridlines, zero baseline requirement.

## Intent
How to adapt data visualizations for live slide presentations, where each slide is on screen only briefly and the audience cannot explore the chart. Use when building charts for projected/presented decks; the principle is clarity and glanceability over exploration depth.

## Guidance (paraphrased, every rule stated on the page)
- Left-rail subhead: "Clarity and simplicity".
- Charts in presentations should be simplified because the audience is live.
- A slide is typically visible for only a few moments, so the chart must be pared down and its message made obvious / readable at a glance.
- Simplify and focus: build focus around a specific data point or region that pulls attention and frames the message; slides usually stay up for under a minute, so there is no time to explore the chart.
- Dark backgrounds: think about the room; in a dimmed room, switch to dark slide backgrounds so slides are not overly bright.
- Do not put many visualizations on a single slide; even simple charts overwhelm and intimidate when shown together.
- Keep accompanying text short and punchy; limit text next to charts so viewers can take in the visual without feeling overloaded.

## Examples marked Recommended
- "Simplify and focus": multi-series line chart titled roughly "US primary energy consumption, 2016–2018 (Quadrillion BTU)"; one series is emphasized (black) against light grey others, a shaded vertical band marks the period of interest, and a magnifier-style circle enlarges an annotation "Oil ramp up" with a short explanation (oil consumption ramped up toward end of 2017 as an effect of US tariff policy changes; original spells "tarifs") — works because attention is directed to one event and the takeaway is stated.
- "When to use dark backgrounds": dark navy slide titled "Once again Apple's customers feel rewarded the most", subtitle "Customer satisfaction rating, 2017 (%)"; three progress rings with large centered values 49% (Facebook), 75% (Amazon), 90% (Apple) — works in dim rooms; the highlighted item (Apple) uses bright blue ring, others white/grey.

## Examples marked Try to avoid / Not recommended
- "Too many visualizations at once": a single slide combining a bubble scatter plot (many colored bubbles of varying size), a column chart (dark navy columns with one bright blue column), and two progress rings (75%, 90%) plus a small legend above the columns — fails because multiple charts at once overwhelm and intimidate.
- "Overcrowding slides with text": slide with a title, two dense columns of body text (placeholder lines) on the left and a stacked column chart on the right — fails because heavy text competes with the visual and overwhelms the viewer.

## Visual specifics observed
- Color roles: in the line example, black = focus series, light grey = context series, light grey shaded band = focus period. Dark slide: dark navy background, white text, white/grey rings for comparators, bright blue ring for the highlighted winner (Apple). Avoid examples use a mixed palette of dark navy, teal, bright blue, cyan, light blue, lavender — many hues simultaneously.
- Axes/gridlines: line chart has light, minimal axis with small tick labels (quarter labels along top, e.g. Q1 '17, Q2, Q3, Q4, Q1 '18, Q2); left axis labelled "BTU" with small ticks 0–4. Gridlines very faint or absent.
- Labels: direct annotation next to the line ("Oil ramp up" bold label + short grey body text); ring values as large centered numbers with category label underneath; no legend in recommended examples. The avoid example relies on a legend (dots + dashes at top right).
- Number formats: percentages with % sign, no decimals.
- Rings: thin stroke circle, partial arc proportional to value.
- Typography: section eyebrow small sans ("Format"); page title large serif ("Presentations"); card headings medium sans; body/callout text small sans grey. Ring numbers appear in serif. Dark slide title sans white, subtitle small grey.
- Recommended/Try to Avoid markers: blue circle check vs dark circle X icons, followed by bold label and grey explanatory sentence.

## Annotation & narrative patterns
- Dark-slide headline is insight-style ("Once again Apple's customers feel rewarded the most") with a descriptive subtitle containing metric, year, and unit in parentheses.
- Line chart title descriptive with date range and unit in parentheses.
- Callout pattern: bold short label + 2–4 line explanation placed beside the relevant data; zoom/magnifier used to show callout legibility.
- No source/footnote visible in examples.

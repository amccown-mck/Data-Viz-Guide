Distribution

Strip and box plot

Distribution charts show how a measure spreads across people, teams or sites: what is typical, how wide the range is, and where the tails sit.

Average compa-ratio is {{text:ds_all_mean}}, yet {{text:ds_focus_low}} of pharmacy technicians are below 0.90. A strip plot shows every person as a dot; the box marks the middle half.

McKesson spec

  • Two to eight groups, each n ≥ {{text:ds_min_n}}, n shown
  • Grey translucent dots; the focus group blue
  • Zone shaded; reference line labelled
  • Anonymous dots: no names, IDs or values
  1. 1Insight headlineThe share in the tail, and how it compares.
  2. 2SubtitleDefines the measure, scope and date.
  3. 3Focus group in blueEach dot one employee; overlaps read as density.
  4. 4Grey contextEvery other group, the same grey.
  5. 5Box on topMiddle 50% of people; the thick line is the median.
  6. 6Zone and referenceShaded below 0.90; the midpoint dashed and named.
  7. 7Share and nThe number the headline uses, at the row end.
  8. 8Keep the value axisIts ticks are how positions are read; no gridlines.
  9. 9Footnote and sourceExplain the dots and the box.

Histogram

Bars that count how many people fall in each equal-width bin. A histogram shows the shape of one group: skew, a long tail, two peaks.

Use it for one population in detail: span of control across all people managers, days to fill across all requisitions. Here the median manager has {{text:ds_span_median}} direct reports, but the tail runs to {{text:ds_span_max}}.

McKesson spec

  • Equal bins that touch, with a thin white seam
  • A bin edge exactly on the policy line
  • One bin per value for counts; 15–40 bins otherwise
  • Bins past the threshold in blue, the rest grey
  1. 1Insight headlineHow many people are past the line.
  2. 2SubtitlePopulation, date, and what the bars count.
  3. 3Bins that matterPast the limit, in blue.
  4. 4Grey contextThe rest of the distribution.
  5. 5The limit on a bin edgeDashed navy, named in place.
  6. 6The share, written outComputed with the same rule as the colors.
  7. 7One bin per valueWhole numbers; bins touch.
  8. 8Light count gridlinesNo axis line; the baseline stays.
  9. 9Footnote and sourceDefine who counts.

Distribution

Distribution variants

Pick the form by how many groups you compare and how many people are in them.

When a distribution is the wrong chart

  • One value per group (median pay by family): a bar or dot plot
  • How the medians shifted between two dates: a dumbbell
  • Two measures per person: a scatter, or a binned heatmap for thousands
  • Groups under {{text:ds_min_n}} people: merge them or leave them out

Box plot

For many groups or tens of thousands of people. Whiskers at the 10th and 90th percentiles, footnoted.

{{exhibit:ds_var_box}}

Range bars

A simpler box for executive decks: the middle 80% as a bar, the median as a tick. Pharmacy technicians start at {{text:ds_focus_p10}}.

{{exhibit:ds_var_range}}

Beeswarm

Dots that never overlap, so every person is countable. Works up to about 300 per row; above that, jitter.

{{exhibit:ds_var_swarm}}

Small-multiple histograms

Three to six groups whose shapes differ. Same bins, same scale, shown as a share of each group.

{{exhibit:ds_var_ridge}}

Distribution best practice

The spread is the message; protect it, and protect the people in it.

Show the spread, not just the average. Group averages look alike; the tails below 0.90 do not.

A bin edge on the threshold. Wide bins that straddle 0.90 color the wrong people: {{text:ds_bins_avoid_share}} instead of {{text:ds_bins_fix_share}}.

One scale for every panel. Rescaled panels make every group look the same shape.

No group under {{text:ds_min_n}}. A dot in a team of four is a findable person. Merge small groups and show n.

Distribution

Distribution best practice, continued

Four more, for strip and box plots in particular.

Jitter and fade. Opaque dots on one line turn into a bar. Spread them across the row and make them translucent.

Explain the box. Few readers know 1.5 × IQR whiskers. Use the 10th–90th percentile and say so.

Don't clip the tails. A tight axis silently drops people ({{text:ds_clipped}} here). Widen it, or cap it and footnote how many.

Sort by median. Put the focus group next to the zone, not where the alphabet puts it.

Build distribution charts

Set the bins and the jitter yourself, not the software. Tested code and click-by-click steps are on the cookbook page.

distribution.md

Before you send it

  • Form matches the number of groups
  • Equal bins; an edge on the threshold
  • n for every group; none under {{text:ds_min_n}}
  • No individual's value in labels or tooltips
  • One x scale across rows and panels
  • Box footnoted; nothing clipped
PowerPoint and Excel

Histogram: make your own bin column and count with COUNTIFS, then a clustered column with Gap Width 0% and white 0.75 pt borders; bins past the threshold in Focus blue. Box plot: Insert › Statistic Chart › Box and Whisker, with outlier points and mean markers off. Strip: XY scatter with y = group index + RAND() jitter, then Paste Values so it stays fixed.

Power BI

Histogram: New group › Bin type: Size of bins, a clustered column chart with inner padding 0, colors by fx › Rules, and the threshold as an X-axis constant line. No native box plot: use the certified Box and Whisker visual or a scatter with a jitter column. Turn tooltips off for individual points.

Python

matplotlib with mckviz: fixed-seed jitter, translucent dots, the box drawn on top.

rng = np.random.default_rng(1)                # fixed jitter: reproducible
for y, fam in enumerate(order):               # groups with n >= 10 only
    x = df.compa_ratio[df.job_family == fam]
    ax.scatter(x, y + rng.uniform(-0.28, 0.28, len(x)), s=7, alpha=0.45,
               color=C.focus if fam == focus else C.context, lw=0)
ax.axvspan(0.68, 0.90, color=C.surface_subtle, zorder=0)
HTML

Plotly: pre-bin with np.histogram and draw go.Bar so you control the edges; go.Box with boxpoints: "all" for strips, and hoveron: "boxes" so no one can hover a single person's pay.

R

geom_jitter() under geom_boxplot(fill = NA, outlier.shape = NA); geom_histogram(breaks = ...) with explicit edges; facet_wrap() keeps one scale.

Big number and KPI card

Single-number charts make one value the message, with the context and comparison that give it meaning.

Use a big number when one value is the finding: an executive summary, a slide opener, a report margin, the header row of a dashboard.

McKesson spec

  • Numeral Bold 700, navy, large
  • One sentence on exactly what is counted
  • Always a comparison; "pts" for point changes
  • Source and as-of date on the card itself
  1. 1Insight headlineThe movement and the gap that remains.
  2. 2SubtitleMetric, scope, date and the base.
  3. 3The numberLarge and Bold, rounded hard.
  4. 4One sentenceCounts make the % concrete.
  5. 5ComparisonSigned, in pts, green and ▲ because it's good news.
  6. 6Gap to targetOrange text because it's worse, said in words.
  7. 7Progress bar0–100% track, target tick labelled.
  8. 8Footnote and sourceDefine the measure; date it.

Progress ring

A ring shows one share of one whole moving toward a target: skills profiles completed, required learning done, plan positions filled.

It works only on a 0–100% scale. If the value can pass 100% or go negative, show the big number and the delta instead.

McKesson spec

  • Starts at 12 o'clock, runs clockwise
  • Blue arc, sand remainder; hole about 80%
  • No rounded caps, gaps, gradients or 3D
  • Target as a thin tick, labelled
  1. 1Insight headlineWhere we are and how far to go.
  2. 2SubtitleMetric, date and the base.
  3. 3Start at 12Then clockwise, like a clock.
  4. 4Blue arc, sand trackThe remainder stays visible.
  5. 5Number in the centerBold; the only centered text.
  6. 6Target tickThin, navy, named.
  7. 7Thin strokeAbout 18% of the radius.
  8. 8Sentence and comparisonLeft-aligned beside the ring.
  9. 9Footnote and sourceWhat "verified" means.

When a single number is the wrong chart

  • The trend is the message: a line, or a sparkline under the number
  • Many groups to compare: bars; eight KPI cards are a bar chart in disguise
  • Several parts of a whole: a donut or stacked bar
  • The base is under {{text:min_n}} people: don't headline it

Single number best practice

A number alone is a fact. Context and comparison make it a finding.

Bold, large, plain. The numeral needs weight, size and space, not boxes or shadows.

Say what is counted. One sentence, with the base: "{{text:sc_base}}".

Always compare. Prior period, target or benchmark, with a sign. Percentage-point changes are "pts", never "%".

No speedometers. Gauges spend ink on a dial and invite red and green. A ring or a bar with a target does the job.

Single number best practice, continued

Four more, from dashboards and scorecards.

Color by judgment, not direction. Green when the change is good, orange when it's worse. Days to fill going up is bad news; pair the color with ▲▼ or words.

Round hard. 62%, 4.3K, $1.2M. Decimals suggest a precision the data doesn't have.

Rings are for 0–100%. Growth, indexes and variances get a big number and a delta.

One anatomy per row. Same order, weight and alignment in every card.

Build a KPI card

A big number is type, not a chart: build it from text wherever you can, so it stays sharp and editable.

kpi-big-number.md

Before you send it

  • Number, sentence and headline agree ({{text:sc_check}})
  • A comparison with a sign; "pts" for points
  • Colors by favorability, plus ▲▼ or words
  • Rings only for 0–100% of one whole
  • No KPI based on fewer than {{text:min_n}} people
PowerPoint and Excel

Big number: a text box in Libre Franklin Bold, 54 pt, Navy #143359, left inset 0; the sentence below at 14 pt; the comparison in Medium, teal #00737A or orange #A75B00. Align left and group. Ring: a Doughnut of value and remainder, Angle of first slice 0°, Hole Size 80%, fills #005A8C / #DEDBD1, no borders, title, legend or labels; lay the number over the center.

Power BI

Card (new): the measure as the callout value (raise it to 40–54; the theme makes it Bold, Segoe UI Bold in the service), Reference labels for the comparison. The KPI visual adds trend and target: set Good #00AF68, Bad #EF8200 and the direction per metric. Rings: a Donut with Inner radius ≈ 80 and a transparent Card on top.

Python

matplotlib Wedge for the ring; Plotly go.Indicator(mode="number+delta") for a KPI row, with increasing / decreasing colors set by favorability.

KPI = mv.TOKENS["typography"]["roles"]["kpi"]        # Bold 700
ax.text(0, 0, f"{value}%", fontsize=KPI["size_pt"] * 0.75,
        fontweight=KPI["weight"], color=C.headline)
txt = f"{mv.signed(value - prior)} pts vs. Mar 2025"   # true minus, explicit +
HTML

Plain HTML text beats a chart library here: <p class="mck-kpi"> from mck-viz.css sets --mck-kpi-size (56 px) and --mck-kpi-weight (700). Color deltas with var(--mck-semantic-positive-text) and var(--mck-semantic-negative-text).

R

annotate("text", ..., fontface = "bold") for the number; a ring with geom_col() + coord_polar(theta = "y", start = 0, direction = 1).

Chart chooser

Pick the chart from the question, not from habit.

  1. 1Write the question the chart answers in one sentence.
  2. 2Find the question type and start with its first choice.
  3. 3Check the traps on the next page.
  4. 4Follow the chart's page to build it.

Every chart name links to its page in this guide. Avoid lists the charts that look right for the question but mislead or slow the reader.

{{exhibit:ch_icon_magnitude}}

Magnitude

How big? Which is bigger, by how much?

Start with
Sorted bar
Also
Dot plot for values far from zero; paired bars
Avoid
Rainbow, 3D or cut-off bars
{{exhibit:ch_icon_part}}

Part-to-whole

What is it made of? How does a total break down?

Start with
100% stacked bar
Also
Waterfall between totals; treemap for many parts
Avoid
Pies over 5 slices
{{exhibit:ch_icon_time}}

Change over time

How is it moving? When did it change?

Start with
Line, focus series in blue
Also
Stacked area; small multiples; bubble timeline
Avoid
Ten colored lines
{{exhibit:ch_icon_ranking}}

Ranking

Who's on top? Who moved?

Start with
Sorted bar; slope for change
Also
Dumbbell for us vs. them; heat table
Avoid
A color for every line
{{exhibit:ch_icon_distribution}}

Distribution

How spread out? What's typical?

Start with
Strip plot or histogram
Also
Box plot to compare many groups
Avoid
An average with no spread
{{exhibit:ch_icon_correlation}}

Correlation

Do two measures move together?

Start with
Scatter or bubble scatter
Also
Heatmap: two categories, one measure
Avoid
Dual axes
{{exhibit:ch_icon_flows}}

Relationships and flows

How do things connect or move?

Start with
Sankey
Also
Parallel coordinates; Venn, 3 sets max
Avoid
A color per stage
{{exhibit:ch_icon_geo}}

Geo-spatial

Where? Does place matter?

Start with
Choropleth for rates
Also
Bubble map for counts; zoom-ins
Avoid
Counts on a choropleth
{{exhibit:ch_icon_single}}

Single number

What one figure should they remember?

Start with
Big number with a comparison
Also
Progress bar or ring for a target
Avoid
Speedometers

Before you build: the common traps

The right chart type can still go wrong. If you are about to do one of these, use the fix instead.

The page numbers refer to this guide's PDF; on screen, each fix links to its page.

About toInstead
Cut the bar axis so small differences showBars from zero; a dot plot for a narrow rangep. {{page:bar-best-practice}}
Give each bar its own colorGrey bars, the one that matters in bluep. {{page:bar-best-practice}}
Put two units on two axesTwo aligned panels, or index both to 100p. {{page:dual-axis}}
Draw ten lines in ten colorsAll grey, one or two in blue; or small multiplesp. {{page:line-best-practice}}
Make a pie with eight slicesA sorted bar; donuts take five parts at mostp. {{page:donut-best-practice}}
Stack eight segments in a barFive at most, the story segment blue on the baselinep. {{page:stacked-best-practice}}
Color a waterfall green up, red downFavorable teal, unfavorable orange; or grey with one bluep. {{page:waterfall-best-practice}}
Map raw counts by stateMap rates; show counts as bubblesp. {{page:maps-best-practice}}
Show a KPI as a speedometerA big number with a comparison, or a bar to targetp. {{page:single-number-best-practice}}
Paste a 30-row table to make one pointChart the point; keep the table for the appendixp. {{page:heat-table}}
Put four charts on one slideTwo at most, each with its own insight headlinep. {{page:two-charts}}
Show a team of 7 peopleSuppress or combine groups under 10p. {{page:people-data}}

Presentations: build it up

The same data needs different treatment on a live slide, a page, a phone or a dashboard. Design for where it will be seen.

In a live meeting, don't reveal a finished chart in one click. Build it in two to five steps so the audience learns the chart before you give them the point.

1 Set the scene. Axes, labels and the data, all grey.

2 Highlight the insight. Color what the headline names.

3 Show the full picture. Add the benchmark and the callout.

Keep the headline, axis and order identical on every step; only the emphasis changes.

Presentations: clarity first

A slide is on screen for less than a minute. There is no time to explore it.

  • With one chart, the slide title is its headline (Bold)
  • One message per chart; at most two charts per slide, never a chart wall
  • Short text beside the chart, or none
  • Native, editable charts from the starter deck, not pictures of charts
  • In a dimmed room, use the dark slide: navy background, light-blue focus

Formats

Two charts on a slide

One chart per slide is the default. When the message needs two pieces of evidence, the titles form a hierarchy.

  1. 1Slide title, BoldThe one message both charts support.
  2. 2Chart headline, SemiBoldEach chart's own finding: still an insight, never a label like "Engagement by job family".
  3. 3Subtitle per chartMetric, unit, scope and period, as always.
  4. 4Aligned and consistentHeadlines on one top line; same rows, same order, same highlight.
  5. 5One source lineShared at the bottom when the charts share sources.

Two is the limit. A third chart means a second slide.

Formats

Flat, not rounded

Rounded pills, soft shadows and gradients date a slide and make it feel less precise. Our look is flat and square.

Square corners. Labels, panels and bars have square corners. No pills or capsules.

No shadows or gradients. Separate panels with white space, a sand fill or a thin rule.

Rules, not boxes. A thin blue rule under a header does the work of a colored capsule.

Words with status dots. A colored dot never stands alone; the word sits beside it.

Formats

Reports and print

On paper the reader sets the pace, so charts can carry more context: full footnotes, more categories, a second chart.

  • Fit the page grid: full page, two-column or one column
  • Export vector (PDF, SVG), or PNG at 300 dpi
  • Nothing below 9 pt; check the printed size, not the screen
  • Grey must survive a black-and-white printer: never rely on color alone

Formats

Web and mobile

A phone is narrow and read at arm's length. Adapt the chart, don't just shrink it.

Resize and reorient. Design for portrait; columns become horizontal bars.

Adjust the hierarchy. When end labels don't fit, use a compact key above the chart and move the insight to the top.

Reduce complexity. One highlighted line, the rest faint; the insight next to it.

Ensure legibility. Fewer text elements, shorter headline, nothing below 11 px.

Formats

Dashboards (Power BI and Fabric)

Dashboards are exploratory, but the same rules apply: grey context, one blue focus, insight titles, minimum group sizes.

  • One question per page; the title states the current answer (a DAX measure can write it)
  • KPI cards top-left: the number, its label, and the comparison
  • Highlights driven by measures, not by clicking bars
  • Minimum n enforced in the measures, so tooltips and drill-throughs inherit it
  • No gauges, pies of many slices, or rainbow defaults

Set-up: docs/powerbi-setup.md and the theme in dist/powerbi/.

Interactivity with a purpose

Interaction lets readers explore at their own pace, but every control must earn its place.

  • The default view tells the story without any clicks
  • Each interaction answers a question the reader will have
  • Tooltips add detail; they never carry the main point
  • Filters and tooltips respect the minimum group size

Cursor tracking

A rule and a tooltip follow the cursor on time series, where reading values off the axis is hard.

Formats

Animation and scrolly-telling

Motion should help the viewer follow the data, never decorate it.

Sequence on purpose. Axes first, then data, then the insight: the build order is the reading order.

Move naturally. Ease in and out; lines draw left to right; bubbles grow from their center. No overshoot or bounce.

Say what you see. Narration and text match what is moving on screen, one point at a time.

Scrolly-telling: the chart stays pinned and updates as the text scrolls. On phones use inline snapshots, and never hijack the scroll.

Formats

Social, infographics and icons

Single-asset graphics are seen out of context, on a phone, in a second.

  • One insight, readable without zooming
  • Self-contained: headline, key number, source and brand mark
  • Three-tone palettes from the tokens: navy with white and light blue
  • Icons only when they help; if it's clear without one, leave it out

Use icons as tally units (one icon = a fixed number of people), never scaled up to show a bigger value.

Relationships and flows

Sankey diagram

Relationship charts show how groups connect: how people move from one state to another, how they profile across many measures, or where groups overlap.

Each transfer is a band whose width is proportional to the people in it. Use it for internal mobility or a reskilling pipeline, when a few big flows matter most.

McKesson spec

  • Focus flows in blue; the rest light grey
  • Thin navy node bars; name and total beside each
  • Two to four stages, eight nodes a column at most
  • No flow under {{text:min_n}} people: merge into "Other"
  1. 1Insight headlineWhere the volume comes from, with the numbers.
  2. 2SubtitleWhat flows, over what period, in what unit.
  3. 3Width = peopleEvery band on one shared scale.
  4. 4Focus flows in blueThe paths the headline names, drawn on top.
  5. 5Light grey contextVisible, but quiet.
  6. 6NodesThin navy bars, name and total, no boxes.
  7. 7Stage headersBold, above each column.
  8. 8Ordered to untangleLargest sources first; destinations follow their source.
  9. 9Footnote and sourceDefine a move; state the minimum flow.

Sankey best practice

Color, shape and the n-size rule decide whether a Sankey can be read.

Color the story. One dark color makes crossing paths impossible to follow. Blue for the focus, light grey for the rest.

Color transfer type, not stage. The stages are already obvious. Give each type of transfer one color at every step; orange only for the loss.

Keep it organic. Curved, semi-transparent bands and thin node bars. No boxed values, no angular arrows.

No flow under {{text:min_n}} people. A three-person move identifies people. Merge small flows into "Other".

Parallel coordinates

One vertical axis per measure, one line per group. It shows a profile: the shape a group makes across four to eight measures at once.

The axes are different measures, not points in time, so their order is a design choice. Put the story first, and orient every axis so up means the same thing.

McKesson spec

  • One or two groups in blue; every other line grey
  • Each axis scaled min–max, real min and max printed
  • Reversed axes labelled; "up = worse" in the subtitle
  • Every line a group of at least {{text:min_n}} people
  1. 1Insight headlineWhat the profile shows, measure by measure.
  2. 2SubtitleGroups, measures, scaling and direction.
  3. 3Story axes firstBold names; related measures side by side.
  4. 4Focus lines in blueHeavier, on top, named at the right end.
  5. 5Grey contextThe other families, thin and unnamed.
  6. 6Real range at the endsMax on top, min below, with units.
  7. 7Reversed, and said soSo up always means worse.
  8. 8One calloutThe exception worth a sentence.
  9. 9Footnote and sourceScaling, reversals, group sizes.

Parallel coordinates best practice

The chart works only when one group stands out from a quiet background.

Use it instead of a dense table. A grid of 40 numbers hides the profile; lines show it at a glance.

One group in color. A shade of blue per line is a tangle with no message.

Scale each axis. On one raw scale, the measure with the biggest numbers flattens the rest.

Point every axis the same way. Mixed directions make a consistently bad group zigzag.

Interactive versions can brush, reorder and hide axes; that beats cropping a static chart to two or three axes.

Venn diagram

Overlapping circles show who sits in more than one group at once: people with several verified skills, mentors who also lead networks.

Use it for two or three sets when the overlap itself is the point. The circles don't encode size; the counts do. For four or more sets, or precise comparison, use combination bars.

McKesson spec

  • One blue that darkens with each level of overlap
  • Equal, regular circles; exact fills, no transparency
  • Exclusive counts in each region; descriptions outside
  • No region under {{text:min_n}} people
  1. 1Insight headlineThe overlap that matters, against the total.
  2. 2SubtitleWhat the sets are, who is counted, when.
  3. 3Single sets, lightestBold name, count beneath.
  4. 4Two-way overlapsA darker blue; the count only.
  5. 5The center, darkestNavy with white text.
  6. 6Descriptions outsideA bold title and a thin leader line.
  7. 7Equal circlesNot scaled to headcount; say so.
  8. 8Footnote and sourceDefine the sets; counts are exclusive.

Relationships and flows

Venn best practice

Keep the shapes simple and let the shading carry the logic.

Darker as sets overlap. An overlap lighter than its sets reads backwards.

Exact fills, one hue. Translucent circles in different colors blend into muddy, off-brand tones.

Three sets at most. Four ellipses make fifteen regions. Switch to combination bars.

Text outside. Sentences inside the circles hide the shading and can't be read.

Build relationship charts

These charts are not native everywhere. Where a tool can't draw one, the cookbook says what to do instead.

sankey.md · parallel-coordinates.md · venn.md

Before you send it

  • Sankey flows conserved; exits are named nodes
  • One color per transfer type; story in blue
  • Axes scaled and oriented the same way
  • Venn overlaps darker; counts exclusive
  • No flow, line or region under {{text:min_n}} people
PowerPoint and Excel

Sankey: no native chart. Build it in Python or HTML, export an SVG, then Insert › Pictures and Convert to Shape to edit labels; a linear pipeline is a native Funnel. Parallel coordinates: normalize in a helper table, Insert › 2-D Line, Switch Row/Column so measures are the categories, vertical gridlines as axes, focus series moved last. Venn: three Ovals; Merge Shapes › Intersect for each overlap; fills B6E2FA, 009EFF, 143359.

Power BI

No built-in Sankey: the AppSource Sankey visual only if your tenant has approved it; group small flows into "Other" in Power Query first. Parallel coordinates as a native Line chart with the measure on the X axis and a normalized DAX measure. No Venn visual: show the combinations as a sorted bar.

Python

Plotly go.Sankey for flows; matplotlib with mckviz for parallel coordinates and the Venn (polygon intersections, exact fills).

link_color = {"progressed": mv.rgba(C.focus, 0.55),       # one color per transfer type
              "dropped": mv.rgba(C.context, 0.45), "not_placed": mv.rgba(C.negative, 0.65)}
go.Sankey(node=dict(color=C.total, thickness=6),
          link=dict(source=src, target=dst, value=people,
                    color=[link_color[t] for t in kind]))
HTML

Plotly Sankey with semi-transparent links; Parcoords lets readers brush and reorder axes; the Venn as filled path shapes with hover off.

R

ggalluvial::geom_alluvium() for Sankey-style flows; geom_line() over min–max scaled long data for parallel coordinates; sf::st_intersection() for Venn regions.
